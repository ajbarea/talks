Why did an M 7.7 injure 27 people in Panama while an M 6.8 doublet at sea went almost unnoticed?

I mapped all 2,016 M2.5+ earthquakes USGS recorded from 25 September to 25 October 2026 in QGIS, then played the month as a time-lapse. Color and size both encode magnitude: minor (2.5 to 3.9), moderate (4.0 to 5.4) and strong to major (5.5+). Each quake stays on the map for 36 hours, so aftershock sequences show up as clusters along fault lines.

Then I stop on four events and compare what the map shows with what happened on the ground: the M 7.7 strike-slip mainshock in Panama, the M 6.7 and M 6.8 doublet on the Mid-Atlantic Ridge, the M 7.0 normal-fault quake on the Tibetan Plateau, and the M 6.9 off Vanuatu with the M 6.5 deep under the Bonin Islands. Shaking estimates and PAGER alerts come from USGS. Damage and casualty figures come from news reports, cited on screen.

An AI avatar of me narrates this video, with my voice. The analysis, maps and script are mine. Made for ISTE-782 Visual Analytics at RIT.
