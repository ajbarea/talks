Twelve courses. Sixteen months. 4.0 GPA, all A's.
M.S. Software Engineering at RIT, officially done. 🤓

Compressed it by stacking a full summer term (three courses including Software Architecture, Foundations of AI, and Cloud Systems) between the standard fall/spring/fall sequence.

The industry-to-research jump has been sharper than I expected. Capstone with Dr. Leon Reznik's group on federated learning. The start of a research line: first paper accepted at IEEE Intelligent Systems (IntelliFL: Metacognition, camera-ready just in), with more under review and in submission across CCNC, DSN, and IJCAI-ECAI.

Next chapter: incoming Computing and Information Sciences PhD at RIT this fall, with a funded research assistantship.

What changed during the Masters: the cadence.
Stopped optimizing for ticket throughput. Started optimizing for what the field will still care about in a year. Slower thinking. Sharper questions. More careful engineering.

Grateful to the RIT SE faculty and to Dr. Reznik's group.
Onward.

Anyone else made the engineer-to-researcher jump? What flipped it for you?
